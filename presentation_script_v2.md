## Opening (0:00 – 1:00)

[Slide 1 – Title: D'Vync Screenless Smartwatch]

Good morning, professor, and good morning, everyone.

Let me start with a quick question. How many times has your phone or your smartwatch interrupted you since this class started? [Pause for 2 seconds.]

Research by Dr. Gloria Mark at the University of California, Irvine shows that after just one interruption, our brain needs on average 23 minutes to fully regain focus.

Now imagine a device that tracks your health 24/7 – your heart, your sleep, your recovery – but never interrupts you, not even once. That is D'Vync, a screenless smartwatch. Its promise is simple: "Made to be worn. Made to be forgotten."

[Slide 2 – Agenda]

In the next thirteen minutes, I will walk you through three parts and a live demo. First, the industry background and the problem we found. Second, our business model – who we sell to and how we make money. And third, how we build our online presence and market the product.

## Part 1: Industry background (1:00 – 3:30)

[Slide 3 – Market size]

Let's start with the market. The global wearable market is expected to exceed 103 billion US dollars by 2026, and wrist devices made up more than half of all revenue in 2025. But screen-based smartwatches are reaching saturation: prices keep rising, and the products look more and more alike. So the industry is moving towards "ambient computing" – technology that works quietly in the background.

In Vietnam, the opportunity is even clearer. The smartwatch and wearable market was worth 252 million dollars in 2025, and it is projected to reach 1.08 billion dollars by 2034. That is a growth rate of 17.6% per year.

[Slide 4 – The consumer paradox]

Two trends drive this growth. The first is a metabolic health crisis. Vietnam has the fastest-growing overweight and obesity rate in Southeast Asia, so urban consumers are shifting from treating illness to preventing it. The second is digital fatigue. We are tired of notifications, screens and constant buzzing on our wrist.

So consumers face a paradox. They need continuous health data, but they also want a digital detox. D'Vync solves this paradox by removing the screen completely.

[Slide 5 – Three tiers of competitors]

When we look at competitors, we see three tiers.

Tier 1 is mainstream smartwatches like Apple Watch and Garmin. Their bright screens use 30 to 50% of the battery, so the sensors only turn on from time to time, and the health data has gaps.

Tier 2 is budget bands like Xiaomi. They are affordable, but less accurate, especially during exercise.

Tier 3 is the screenless pioneers, Whoop and Oura. They have the right idea, but they are expensive, their subscriptions are costly, and they are not built around Asian users.

[Slide 6 – D'Vync's unique value]

This is where D'Vync fits. With no screen to power, our sensors can run continuously at 50 to 100 Hz for 5 to 7 days on a single charge. That continuous data allows an accurate measurement of heart rate variability – HRV – during deep sleep, which is the key indicator of recovery.

In one sentence: zero distractions, and more complete health data.

## Part 2: Business model (3:30 – 7:00)

[Slide 7 – Business model]

Now, our business model. D'Vync is a B2C e-commerce business combined with m-commerce. Customers discover and buy the device on our website, then use the mobile app to view their data and upgrade their plan.

It is a hybrid model. On one side, we are a manufacturer selling directly to consumers. There are no distributors, so we keep the margin, control the brand experience and own our customer data. On the other side, we are a service provider: the app delivers health analysis as a subscription.

And there is a clear market gap. Whoop, our closest reference product, has no official distributor in Vietnam, so users buy hand-carried devices with limited warranty. D'Vync fills that gap with local pricing, local support and data baselines suited to Asian users.

[Slide 8 – Two customer groups]

We target two customer groups. The first is athletes and fitness enthusiasts. They need accurate data on sleep, recovery and HRV to plan their training, and a light, durable device with no glass to break.

The second is minimalists and classic watch lovers – office workers who love their mechanical watch and don't want another screen. They can wear D'Vync on the other wrist: no lights, no vibrations, no notifications.

Different people, but the same need: reliable data from a device that does not get in the way.

[Slide 9 – Value Proposition Canvas]

Using the Value Proposition Canvas: the customer pains are daily charging that breaks sleep data, distracting notifications, and bulky designs. The gains we offer are 5 to 7 days of battery, continuous sleep and recovery tracking, AI-personalized advice, and a minimalist look.

[Slide 10 – Revenue model]

We make money in two ways. First, hardware. The device costs 2,490,000 dong including VAT – the same price as the Xiaomi Smart Band 10 Pro Ceramic, and half the price of a Garmin Forerunner 165. The hardware gross margin is about 43%.

Second, a freemium subscription. Basic metrics like heart rate, steps and sleep duration are free. Premium costs 99,000 dong per month or 990,000 dong per year, and adds AI analysis, recovery recommendations and long-term trend reports. Premium has a gross margin of 60 to 67%.

So for a customer who buys the device and stays on the annual plan, our blended margin grows from about 47.5% in year one to about 52% over three years. That is why keeping customers matters as much as the first sale.

[Slide 11 – Cost of ownership vs competitors]

Compared with competitors, a D'Vync customer pays about 3.5 million dong in the first year. That is roughly a third less than Whoop, and about two-thirds less than Oura Ring 4. From the second year, the gap gets even wider, because our Premium fee is only about half of Oura's.

[Slide 12 – SWOT and team]

Of course, we want to be realistic. Our strengths are long battery life, uninterrupted data and local pricing. Our weakness is that we are a new brand with high upfront investment. Our opportunities are the demand for preventive health, the digital detox trend and future telehealth. And the threats are big brands copying us, regulations on medical devices and personal data, and resistance to subscription fees.

To manage this, our lean team is built on four pillars: product and engineering, data and AI, market and customers, and legal and compliance. Compliance is included from day one, because we handle personal health data.

## Part 3: Online presence and marketing (7:00 – 9:00)

[Slide 13 – E-commerce presence map]

Finally, our online presence. We have three touchpoints, and each has a clear role. Social media – TikTok, Instagram Reels and Facebook – builds awareness and community. The website is our direct sales channel: it explains the product and turns visitors into buyers. And the app delivers value after the purchase, which drives Premium subscriptions, renewals and referrals.

In short: the website wins the first sale, and the app keeps the customer.

[Slide 14 – Website prototype. Keep this slide short; the live site comes in the demo after Slide 15.]

Our website is where visitors become buyers. It is built to be simple and easy to use on a phone, because many visitors arrive from social media. I will show you the live site in a moment.

[Slide 15 – Marketing funnel]

Our marketing follows the customer journey.

At the awareness stage, fitness creators talk about sleep and recovery, while lifestyle creators show the discreet design.

At the consideration and conversion stage, each video leads to the matching page on our website. The free tier and a one-month Premium trial lower the risk of trying.

At the activation and retention stage, simple app onboarding helps users pair the device, and weekly and monthly reports keep them engaged.

We measure every stage: traffic and acquisition cost, checkout completion, device pairing, and trial-to-paid and renewal rates.

## Conclusion (12:00 – 13:00)

[Slide 17 – Conclusion]

To wrap up. Wearables are moving from screens to ambient computing, and Vietnamese consumers want health data without digital stress.

D'Vync answers that with three things: a screenless device that tracks continuously for up to a week; a hybrid hardware-plus-subscription model, priced for the local market; and an online strategy where the website wins the first sale and the app keeps the customer.

Looking ahead, AI will turn raw numbers into simple, daily advice, and our data could one day connect with telehealth services and doctors.

Our goal is to be an invisible guardian on your wrist – a device you can forget, because it never stops looking after you.

Thank you for listening. We are happy to take your questions.
